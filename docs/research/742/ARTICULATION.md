# Articulation contract

The browser exposes five bounded presentation channels plus a three-mode steering selector.

1. Boom lift rotates the three-section boom around the reconstructed rear chassis pivot, between a reconstructed zero-degree ground-clear stow and 69 degrees. Zero degrees places the neutral fork load surface at 0.388 m and the complete rear-plane-to-tip length at 6.9772 m. The maximum-reach presentation uses a separately selected reconstructed 3-degree pose; no official source establishes that angle as the manufacturer maximum-reach datum. The 69-degree endpoint is visible on the published chart. The browser does not reproduce chart capacities.
2. Telescope translates the second section 3.604 m and the chain-driven third section an additional 3.604 m along the boom axis. The 3.604 m telescope-cylinder stroke is evidence-derived; the exact owned section origins and chain transfer geometry remain reconstruction. At full extension the solver retains 1.826 m base/mid and 1.626 m mid/fly visual overlap, and the upper surface of level forks reaches 12.7989 m at 69 degrees against the published 12.8 m maximum lift height.
3. Carriage tilt keeps the standard floating carriage controllable around its boom-head pivot. The asymmetric reconstructed range is 5 degrees down and 12 degrees up. Across the full sampled combination of lift, telescope, tilt, frame level, steering, and modes, the lowest fork blade remains 0.1656 m above the flat-floor proxy. The neutral stow has a 0.328 m blade bottom and 0.388 m load surface.
4. Steering is solved from one translated opposed-rod rack and two invariant steering bars per axle, not from imposed wheel angles. The evidence-derived service limit is 55 degrees at the inner wheel; at maximum command the reconstructed linkage yields a 27.397-degree outer wheel. Circle mode translates the rear rack oppositely, crab translates both racks together, and front mode leaves the rear rack neutral. The linkage is a visual Ackermann approximation with a measured maximum relative center-fit error of 10.47 percent across continuous travel, not factory calibration. At maximum circle command the owned geometry produces a 3.7148 m outside wheel-center path; the posed GLB tire/body envelopes are reported separately. The published 3.66 m outside turning radius is not equated to any of those values because its reference locus and swept-envelope convention are unresolved.
5. Frame level rolls the chassis/boom/cab branch around a reconstructed longitudinal axis at 0.82 m visual height up to the published 10-degree limit while the ground-running-gear branch remains fixed. That display axis is not claimed to be an axle centerline or a published JLG coordinate.

Each axle is presented as one fixed-length double-ended steering barrel with two straight opposed rods, an invariant 1.4 m rod-joint span, and separate 0.491477 m rigid steering bars to the moving steering joints; no duplicate hydraulic actuator is shown. Every other cylinder also keeps a fixed visual barrel while only rod exposure changes between its gland and moving pin. One continuous extend-chain assembly is presented as two parallel fixed-length paths, while the single retract path is separately routed. All three use tangent straight legs and polygonal near-180-degree U-wraps outside their moving sheaves. A 2,001-step continuous travel sweep measures less than 8e-15 m total-length drift, zero normalized tangent-dot error, at least 0.003636 m sheave-surface clearance after chain radius, and a maximum 0.003604 m adjacent endpoint step. Exact chain lengths, pitch, sheave radii, and anchor coordinates remain reconstructed.

The boom-angle sensor is presented as a fixed rotary body, an invariant 0.10 m crank, and an invariant 0.42 m ball-jointed connecting rod solved by circle intersection to the moving first-section pin. Those dimensions and pin coordinates remain reconstructed. The axle housings are transverse, with a central simplified differential cue and short longitudinal input flange. The minimum named rigid-underbody proxy clearance in the actual GLB is 0.4600 m; the published 0.432 m ground-clearance value does not identify an exact component locus, so this is an approximate visual acceptance rather than a differential-clearance claim.

The visual pin-distance deltas reconcile to the cited strokes: lift 1.070000/1.070 m, telescope 3.604000/3.604 m, compensation 0.278000/0.278 m, head tilt 0.387999/0.388 m, and frame sway 0.167996/0.168 m. The rear-axle-stabilization cue uses only 0.105582 m of the published 0.200 m cylinder stroke because the explorer shows frame-level-induced endpoint motion only; it does not simulate free, slow, or locked RAS states.

`machines/742/solver.js` is the single executable pose authority. Browser articulation imports it directly; Blender build/render and the Python validator invoke it through `scripts/solve_742_pose.mjs`. The validator actually executes 416,745 distinct input states across lift, telescope, tilt, frame level, steering input, and all three steering modes, plus the 2,001-state all-chain and steering sweeps. It asserts fixed barrels, positive rod exposure, invariant rigid links, chain length/tangency/clearance, posed-GLB hierarchy, actual tread-plane reach, named rigid-underbody clearance, exact runtime stow length, maximum-height level forks, the selected 3-degree/full-extension reach pose, and evidence stroke usage. `scripts/validate_742_posed_glb.py` additionally imports and poses the actual binary through the same bridge; it measured 12.7989 m maximum-lift fork surface, 8.8671 m reach from the actual front tread plane, level forks at both presets, and explicit reconstructed tire/body turning envelopes. This is geometry continuity evidence—not manufacturer dynamics, load, stability, interlock, service, or safety evidence.

The stow command resets lift, telescope, carriage tilt, steering, and frame level. The showcase command animates a deterministic display sequence. Neither command represents operating procedure.
