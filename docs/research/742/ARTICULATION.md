# Articulation contract

The browser exposes five bounded presentation channels plus a three-mode steering selector.

1. Boom lift rotates the three-section boom around the reconstructed rear chassis pivot, between 3 and 69 degrees. The 69-degree endpoint is visible on the published chart; the browser does not reproduce chart capacities.
2. Telescope translates the second section 3.604 m and the chain-driven third section an additional 3.604 m along the boom axis. The 3.604 m telescope-cylinder stroke is evidence-derived; the exact owned section origins and chain transfer geometry remain reconstruction. At full extension the solver retains 1.826 m base/mid and 1.626 m mid/fly visual overlap, and the upper surface of level forks reaches 12.7989 m at 69 degrees against the published 12.8 m maximum lift height.
3. Carriage tilt keeps the standard floating carriage controllable around its boom-head pivot. The asymmetric reconstructed range is 5 degrees down and 12 degrees up. Down-tilt is deliberately bounded so both fork blades retain at least 0.452 m of flat-floor clearance across every sampled lift, telescope, tilt, and plus/minus 10-degree frame-level combination.
4. Steering uses nonlinear wheelbase/track Ackermann geometry rather than a constant inner/outer multiplier. The evidence-derived service limit is 55 degrees at the inner wheel. With the 3.42 m published wheelbase and 2.1005 m temporary-reference wheel-center track, symmetric circle steering produces a 27.5-degree outer wheel; front-only steering produces 37.3 degrees. Circle mode assigns opposite front/rear axle angles about one instantaneous center, crab uses four parallel wheels, and front mode leaves the rear wheels neutral. The resulting 3.7148 m outside wheel-center radius is a visual cross-check against the rounded published 3.66 m outside turning radius, not a manufacturer steering/dynamics calibration.
5. Frame level rolls the chassis/boom/cab branch around a reconstructed axle-height longitudinal pivot up to the published 10-degree limit while the ground-running-gear branch remains fixed. The 0.82 m pivot height is a visual hierarchy choice, not a published JLG coordinate.

Each axle is presented as one double-ended steering cylinder with two opposed rods attached directly to the steering joints; no duplicate tie rod is shown. Visible lift, telescope, compensation-master, carriage-tilt-slave, frame-sway, rear-axle-stabilization, and steering barrel/rod assemblies are endpoint-rigged to the same presentation state. The two extend-chain cues and single retract-chain cue, plus the boom hose/tube cues, run inside or below the boom rather than as exposed top bars. Endpoint closure is sampled through motion, but exact anchors and flexible routing remain reconstructed.

The visual pin-distance deltas reconcile to the cited strokes: lift 1.0699/1.070 m, telescope 3.604/3.604 m, compensation 0.2786/0.278 m, head tilt 0.3877/0.388 m, and frame sway 0.1680/0.168 m. The rear-axle-stabilization cue uses only 0.1056 m of the published 0.200 m cylinder stroke because the explorer shows frame-level-induced endpoint motion only; it does not simulate free, slow, or locked RAS states.

The validator samples 416,745 distinct input states across lift, telescope, tilt, frame level, steering input, and all three steering modes. It also asserts posed-GLB hierarchy, section overlap, fork clearance, the maximum-height level-fork pose, a separate 3-degree/full-extension maximum-reach pose (8.8700 m at the 24 in load center against the published 8.86 m), actuator stroke usage, and Ackermann center agreement. This is geometry continuity evidence—not manufacturer dynamics, load, stability, interlock, service, or safety evidence.

The stow command resets lift, telescope, carriage tilt, steering, and frame level. The showcase command animates a deterministic display sequence. Neither command represents operating procedure.
